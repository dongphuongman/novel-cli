## Suspense / mystery arc templates

The arc shapes below suit long-form suspense, mystery, police procedural and thriller. Combine them freely to fit your setting; you do not have to use them all.

### Case investigation arc (10–15 chapters)
2–3 chapters of the crime and the scene → 3–5 chapters of investigating multiple angles (each clue opening a new question) → 2–3 chapters of the key breakthrough → 1–2 chapters of the reveal and its aftermath
- Clues must be presented fairly; at the reveal the reader can trace every setup
- No investigative thread should merely "eliminate a suspect" — each must uncover new background
- The emotional impact after the reveal matters more than the deduction itself

### Closing-in-on-the-truth arc (8–12 chapters)
2–3 chapters of an old case resurfacing or a new clue emerging → 3–5 chapters of pursuing it deeper (the protagonist edging toward the core) → 2–3 chapters of escalating danger and a forced choice
- Suits a serial-style long-range mystery (a central puzzle running across several arcs)
- Every step closer to the truth exacts a price (a trust broken, physical danger, a moral bind)
- Reveal part of the truth while raising a larger question

### Manhunt / infiltration arc (12–18 chapters)
2–3 chapters of fixing the target and forming the plan → 6–10 chapters of execution (repeated crises and improvisation) → 2–3 chapters of the final confrontation and an unexpected reversal
- Unplanned situations keep arising during execution, showing the character's ability to adapt
- An infiltration arc needs environmental detail and sustained tension
- The confrontation should not be only physical; it needs a psychological contest and a battle over information

### Trial / disclosure arc (8–12 chapters)
2–3 chapters of assembling evidence and pre-trial manoeuvring → 3–5 chapters of formal engagement (prosecution and defence, interrogation, a public confrontation) → 1–2 chapters of surprise evidence and the final ruling
- Suits a staged resolution in the story (closing the case, a conviction, the truth going public)
- The two sides take turns having the upper hand; it is not one-sided
- The final ruling need not be justice prevailing — it can be a compromise, or a larger conspiracy

### Linked-serial-crimes arc (15–20 chapters)
2–3 chapters of seemingly unrelated cases → 5–8 chapters of finding the link and the pattern → 3–5 chapters of hunting the person behind it → 2–3 chapters of the final showdown and the truth
- Early cases are each distinctive and each stands on its own
- Discovering the link is a major turn that changes how the protagonist understands the whole situation
- The mastermind's motive must be supported by foreshadowing laid earlier

### Everyday / transitional arc (4–6 chapters)
Repairing relationships, a new lead brewing, psychological recovery, advancing a hidden thread
- After a high-pressure arc the story needs room to breathe — but never fully relaxes
- Advancing the hidden thread: the reader can feel that something is about to happen
- Show how the characters have changed psychologically after a major case
