## Romance / relationship arc templates

The arc shapes below suit long-form romance, relationship drama, coming-of-age and contemporary love stories. Combine them freely to fit your setting; you do not have to use them all.

### First meeting / reunion arc (8–12 chapters)
2–3 chapters of each character's own life (establishing them independently) → 3–5 chapters of the pivotal meeting and forced proximity (generating friction and a first impression) → 2–3 chapters of first understanding and a subtle turn
- The first impression is better as a misunderstanding or a clash than as love at first sight
- The premise for the forced proximity must be natural (a collaboration, neighbours, colleagues), not a contrived coincidence
- The arc ends on the realization "this person is not who I thought they were"

### Slow-burn arc (10–15 chapters)
3–4 chapters of deepening everyday interaction (shared experience, mutual help) → 3–5 chapters of a catalysing event (a crisis, a misunderstanding, a third party) → 2–3 chapters at the peak of the ambiguity (an almost-confession, a moment of closeness, an interruption) → 1–2 chapters of confirming the relationship or retreating from it
- This is the arc that most needs detail: micro-expressions, small gestures and unguarded attention work better than long interior monologue
- The warming is not linear; there must be retreat and probing along the way
- A third party (an ex, a rival suitor, someone who breeds misunderstanding) must have their own motive for stepping in

### Misunderstanding / conflict arc (8–12 chapters)
2–3 chapters seeding the friction (differing values, a secret, outside pressure) → 2–3 chapters of the conflict breaking (not the kind of misunderstanding a conversation would fix) → 2–3 chapters of each absorbing it while outside events press → 1–2 chapters of an opening for reconciliation (without a full return to how things were)
- The conflict must reach a real difference in values, not "why didn't you pick up the phone"
- Reconciliation is not one side admitting total fault; both grow and both give ground
- After the conflict the relationship enters a new stage rather than simply reverting

### Separation / testing arc (10–15 chapters)
2–3 chapters on why they are forced apart (distance, family opposition, a career choice) → 5–8 chapters of each facing their own challenge (independent growth alternating with longing) → 2–3 chapters of meeting again and redefining the relationship
- Both sides need an independent growth line during the separation; "missing each other" is not enough
- Keep the longing restrained — express it through concrete behavior (a habit kept, an old relationship measured against a new one) rather than interior monologue
- The reunion is not a return to the past; it is two people who have grown choosing again

### Shared-crisis arc (8–12 chapters)
2–3 chapters of an outside crisis arriving (a family upheaval, a career crisis, a health problem) → 3–5 chapters facing it together (showing the depth and resilience of the bond) → 2–3 chapters of resolution and the relationship deepening
- The crisis must be real and weighty, not melodrama for its own sake
- Facing it together exposes a truer side of each of them
- This suits the middle of a relationship: outside pressure tests the connection inside

### Everyday / sweet arc (5–8 chapters)
Daily life together, interaction within their circle, small events and small surprises, planting foreshadowing
- Sweetness is not the absence of conflict; the conflicts are simply small and quickly resolved
- Everyday detail consolidates the reader's investment in the relationship
- It builds pressure for the next big conflict arc — the sweeter it gets, the more the coming rupture hurts
